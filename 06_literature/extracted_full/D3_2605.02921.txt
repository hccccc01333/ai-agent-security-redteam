A B S T R A C T
As LLMs continue to shape real-world applications, automated jailbreak generation becomes
essential to reveal safety weaknesses and guide model improvement. Existing automatic jailbreak
generation methods have not yet fully considered two important aspects: adaptability to evolving
safety-finetuned models, which affects their effectiveness on newer model versions, and diversity
in generated prompts, which can cause narrow or repetitive attack patterns. To address these
issues, we propose EvoJail, an instruction-fusion-driven evolutionary jailbreak generation frame-
work that formalizes jailbreak prompt generation as a multi-objective black-box optimization
problem and leverages the principles of evolutionary algorithms to search for jailbreak prompts
that can adapt across different model versions and exhibit diverse attack patterns. Specifically,
EvoJail integrates jailbreak prompt generation into an iterative evolutionary loop, where at
each iteration candidate prompts are evaluated directly against the target model and then
selected and varied based on the target model’s responses, enabling the generation process
to continuously adapt to model updates. To enhance diversity, EvoJail introduces field-aware
instruction fusion to construct diverse starting points and incorporates diversity-aware objectives
into the evolutionary fitness function, guiding the search toward prompts with richer semantic
variation, while further designing multi-level LLM-based mutation operators that modify prompt
structures at different granularities to promote structural diversity throughout the evolutionary
process. The performance of EvoJail has been evaluated using five widely-used LLMs from
the perspectives of effectiveness, adaptability, diversity, and efficiency. Results demonstrate that
EvoJail has stronger adaptability and can achieve over 93% attack success rate and more than
5.6% improvement in diversity metrics over state-of-the-art methods. Source code is released at
https://github.com/kaikaikai7/EvoJail.
1. Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have been widely adopted in real-world applications and are now integral to profes-
sional workflows. They are applied across a wide range of fields, including but not limited to medicine (Thirunavukarasu
et al., 2023a), education (Awasthi et al., 2025), finance (Aarab, 2025), supporting tasks such as medical report
analysis (Thirunavukarasu et al., 2023b), teaching plan generation (Hu et al., 2024), and financial decision-making (Li
et al., 2025a). By enabling natural language interaction, LLMs allow users to access information and accomplish tasks
in an intuitive and efficient manner: users only need to state their goals in natural language, and the LLM will deliver
the results much like a capable assistant. These capabilities substantially improve professional efficiency and therefore
have driven their widespread adoption.
However, while LLMs provide unprecedented convenience and productivity, their high level of interactivity also
exposes them to significant safety risks. One prominent example is jailbreak attacks, which refer to inducing an LLM
to produce outputs that it is normally designed to avoid, using carefully designed inputs known as jailbreak prompts.
These attacks are carried out by adversaries who seek to bypass the built-in safety mechanisms of an LLM, causing the
model to generate harmful, prohibited, or otherwise undesirable content (Zou et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2024; Yu et al.,
2024; Deng et al., 2024a; Jiang et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2026; Jabbar et al., 2025). It may in turn enable dangerous

To address these risks, current defense mechanisms often rely on collecting many different jailbreak prompts to

automated and effective methods for generating jailbreak prompts to support safety evaluations and the development
of stronger defenses. Among them, existing methods are developed mainly in black-box settings, where only the input-
output behavior of LLMs is accessible, and are typically implemented with fixed attack strategies such as role-playing,
encrypted communication, prompt rewriting, and text continuation (Ding et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024; Jiang et al.,
2025a; Li et al., 2023a; Yuan et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023b; Chao et al., 2023).
The jailbreak prompts produced by these strategies can achieve high attack success rates (ASR), but the generated
samples may follow limited and repetitive attack patterns, resulting in noticeable structural and semantic similarity
among the samples due to the use of preset templates or fixed transformation rules. Such homogeneity may cause (i)
inadequate adaptability and (ii) insufficient diversity at the set level of jailbreak prompts produced by these methods,
making it unclear whether they remain effective when attack scenarios deviate from the template patterns on which
they rely. In terms of adaptability, as LLMs are continuously updated, newer versions often patch jailbreak prompts
that were effective against earlier releases. Consequently, existing methods may struggle to detect safety vulnerabilities
in updated LLMs. As shown in Figure 1, we tested 1,405 jailbreak prompts compiled in Ref. (Shen et al., 2024) from
real-world scenarios against multiple versions of the ChatGPT and Llama model families, revealing a clear decline in
ASR across successive model versions. Numerous jailbreaks that worked on earlier versions of ChatGPT no longer
work on more recent releases, such as GPT-4o, with only 16 templates remaining effective. A similar phenomenon is
observed across the Llama family.
Meanwhile, in terms of diversity, most existing methods focus on either general-field settings or a small number
of specialized fields, without further exploring their applicability across a broader range of application contexts. As a
result, jailbreaks designed for general-field use may exhibit varying levels of effectiveness in field-specific scenarios,
while those tailored to particular fields may not perform as effectively when applied outside their original field. This,
in turn, can further affect defense mechanisms, as many defenses rely heavily on such jailbreak prompts to construct
safety training datasets, making LLMs prone to overfitting specific jailbreak prompt generation strategies, and leaving
them vulnerable to unseen attack patterns.
Motivated by these observations, our research was guided by the following research questions: (RQ1) How can
we design a jailbreak prompt generation method that adaptively evolves with continuous LLM updates, enabling it
to be consistently effective in probing the safety of newly released LLMs? (RQ2) How can we achieve field-aware

evolutionary generation method for jailbreak prompts. EvoJail formalizes jailbreak prompt generation as a multi-
objective black-box optimization problem over a discrete and structured instruction space, and incorporates the
principles of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) into the prompt generation process. Specifically, EvoJail leverages the
strong exploratory capabilities of EAs (Salimans et al., 2017) to guide the prompt search direction through a customized
fitness function, enabling efficient exploration of the prompt space while maintaining population diversity. The method
consists of a customized population initialization strategy, an evolutionary selection mechanism that integrates a
multi-objective fitness function with an elite-hybrid evolutionary strategy, and LLM-based crossover and mutation
operators. Together, these components enable the automated generation of jailbreak prompts that are structurally
diverse, semantically obfuscated, and highly evasive, allowing them to effectively bypass the defense mechanisms
of modern safety-finetuned LLMs.
Specifically, to address RQ1, EvoJail embeds jailbreak prompt generation into an iterative evolutionary loop in
which candidate prompts are evaluated directly against the current target model in each generation. By grounding
selection and variation in the model’s real-time responses, the search trajectory naturally adapts to changes in model
behavior and defense mechanisms. As a result, jailbreak prompts can be progressively refined and remain effective
even as LLMs undergo continuous safety updates, without relying on fixed prompt patterns. To address RQ2, EvoJail
incorporates diversity-aware constraints throughout the evolutionary process to avoid convergence toward a narrow
set of jailbreak templates. In the initialization stage, it introduces multiple semantic starting points by embedding
adversarial objectives into benign task instructions drawn from diverse application fields. During evolution, EvoJail
further employs a multi-objective fitness function that jointly optimizes attack effectiveness and prompt diversity,
encouraging broad coverage across different fields and linguistic expressions. Combined with LLM-based crossover
and mutation operators, EvoJail is enabled to continuously generate structurally and semantically diverse jailbreak
prompts.
Experimental results show that EvoJail achieves over 93% ASR against cutting-edge LLMs including Llama-3.3-
70B, GPT-4o, outperforming all baselines. After model updates, EvoJail can automatically evolve to generate new
jailbreak prompts, maintaining stable attack performance, and demonstrating exceptional evolutionary robustness.
Meanwhile, EvoJail demonstrates highly stable attack performance across different field task types. When embedding
the same malicious objective into various contextual tasks such as finance, education, and healthcare, EvoJail
consistently achieves an ASR greater than 87%, reflecting strong task-agnostic generalization ability. Moreover, in
diversity analysis experiments, EvoJail outperforms baselines by more than 5.6% in diversity metrics while maintaining
a high ASR of 90%. This improvement mainly stems from: (i) the obfuscated-objective prompt initialization, which
expresses malicious goals across diverse task forms; and (ii) the integration of explicit diversity metrics into the multi-
objective fitness function, which drives the evolutionary process toward more semantically and syntactically diverse
prompts, preventing convergence to narrow or repetitive attack patterns.
In summary, our main contributions are listed below:
• We introduce EvoJail, a novel black-box jailbreak generation framework that transforms jailbreak prompt
generation from static template design into an adaptive, diversity-aware optimization process guided by
evolutionary principles.
• We design a multi-objective-guided evolutionary search mechanism in which diverse search starting points are
explicitly constructed via field-level instruction fusion, and the subsequent search is guided by safety-risk and
diversity fitness functions to progressively yield effective and diverse jailbreak prompts.
• We evaluate EvoJail on five safety-trained LLMs, including GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4o, Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-
14B and DeepSeek-R1-14B, achieving more than 93% ASR and over 5.6% improvement in diversity metrics over
strong baselines, demonstrating its superiority in both effectiveness and coverage.

2. Related Work
Enhancing the safety of LLMs against jailbreak attacks requires a deep understanding of jailbreak prompt
generation mechanisms. By actively collecting and analyzing diverse jailbreak prompt samples, researchers can
identify vulnerabilities in LLMs across their safety-related capabilities such as content filtering, intent recognition,
and semantic understanding. Existing jailbreak prompt generation methods can be categorized into black-box and
white-box approaches based on whether access to model parameters is required (Yi et al., 2024).
2.1. White-box Methods
In white-box methods, the attacker has access to the model parameters. White-box methods can be further cate-
gorized based on the type of white-box information exploited by the attacker, including gradient-based methods (Zou
et al., 2023), logits-based methods (Zhang et al., 2023), and fine-tuning-based methods (Qi et al., 2024). Among them,
gradient-based methods involve adding prefixes or suffixes to harmful instructions and then iteratively optimizing the
added text using the model’s gradient feedback, with the goal of inducing the model to produce affirmative responses to
the harmful instructions (Jia et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Andriushchenko et al., 2025; Hayase et al.,
2024). In certain scenarios, attackers may only have access to partial information, such as logits, which represent the
model’s output token probability distribution for each instance. Logits-based methods iteratively modify and optimize
the input prompts until the distribution of output tokens satisfies a specified criterion, thereby generating harmful
responses (Guo et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024). Fine-tuning-based methods
employ harmful data to fine-tune LLMs, thereby amplifying the harmfulness of the models and making them more
susceptible to responding to adversarial prompts (Yang et al., 2024; Lermen and Rogers-Smith, 2024; Zhan et al., 2024;
Xie et al., 2025). White-box methods often require access to model parameters and gradient computation, resulting in
high computational costs and limited practicality.
2.2. Black-box Methods
In contrast, black-box methods do not require access to internal model parameters, making them applicable across
a broader range of practical settings, including scenarios involving commercial models where parameter access is
restricted. At early stages, many studies drew on human experience to design heuristic templates for generating jailbreak
prompts. These approaches are collectively referred to as heuristic jailbreak attacks. They typically exhibit relatively
homogeneous structures and limited variation in linguistic patterns, which can introduce recognizable regularities and
consequently make them more susceptible to detection by pattern-based defense mechanisms. Building on this line
of research, subsequent work has explored evolutionary strategies that iteratively transform and diversify jailbreak
prompts, thereby improving adaptability and reducing structural regularities that may be exploited by defenses. Such
methods are collectively referred to as evolutionary jailbreak attacks. The following paragraphs present a detailed
discussion of these two categories.
Heuristic jailbreak attacks. Heuristic jailbreak attacks typically rely on human prior knowledge to construct jailbreak
templates that can hide the malicious intent of harmful instructions, thereby bypassing the safety mechanisms of
LLMs. One type of method combines harmful instructions with tasks such as psychology and role-playing to construct
deceptive scenarios (Li et al., 2023b; Yao et al., 2024; Kang et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2024; Shah et al., 2023). Another
type of method transforms jailbreak attacks from a zero-shot setting into a few-shot setting by embedding adversarial
examples into the context (Wei et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2024b; Li et al., 2023a; Zheng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b).
Additionally, some studies conduct jailbreak attacks using cryptographic (Yuan et al., 2024; Lv et al., 2024; Jiang
et al., 2024) and low-resource language strategies (Deng et al., 2024c; Li et al., 2024a). Heuristic jailbreak attacks
are relatively inexpensive to implement, but their reliance on fixed structures limits their overall stealth and diversity,
making them easier to detect and defend against.
Evolutionary jailbreak attacks. Evolutionary jailbreak attacks are typically designed as an iterative optimization
process, enabling them to dynamically adapt to the defense mechanisms of the target model and continuously improve
their effectiveness. One type of method simulates biological evolutionary mechanisms such as selection, crossover, and
mutation, iteratively evolving to obtain more effective jailbreak prompts (Liu et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025; Chen et al.,
2025; Li et al., 2024b). Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2024) apply genetic algorithms to jailbreak attack research, optimizing
jailbreak prompts at both the sentence and paragraph levels. SMJ (Li et al., 2024b) models the search process for
prompts that simultaneously satisfy semantic similarity and jailbreak effectiveness as a multi-objective optimization
problem, and employs a standardized genetic algorithm to generate qualified prompts. In contrast, EvoSynth (Chen

While substantial progress has been made in jailbreak prompt generation, two aspects have not yet been fully
explored. The first relates to how jailbreak prompts can adapt to the continuous updates of LLMs. As LLMs
undergo ongoing safety fine-tuning and alignment improvements, static or fixed jailbreak strategies may gradually
lose effectiveness. The second relates to the diversity of jailbreak prompts. Existing attack methods often follow a
limited set of patterns or tasks, which may not sufficiently reflect the wide range of attack strategies encountered in
real-world applications and, consequently, limit the comprehensive evaluation of LLM safety alignment. Motivated by
these observations, this work focuses on addressing both aspects and proposes an evolutionary generation method for
diverse jailbreak prompts.
3. Problem Statement
In this section, we provide a formalization of the jailbreak problem along three dimensions: the knowledge and
capabilities attributed to the adversary, the adversary’s attack goals and success conditions, and the research objectives
that guide the design of our proposed method.
3.1. Adversary Knowledge
We consider a black-box adversary targeting an LLM 𝑀tgt that is accessible only through a public API or interface.
The adversary has no access to the internal parameters, training data, safety policies, or filtering mechanisms of
𝑀tgt. Interaction is restricted to issuing natural-language prompts 𝑝∈and observing the corresponding responses
𝑦= 𝑀tgt(𝑝) ∈. The adversary does not rely on any prior knowledge of the model architecture or implementation,
but can exploit the instruction-following behavior of 𝑀tgt and optionally use surrogate open-source LLMs for offline
optimization. This setting reflects realistic attack scenarios against commercial closed LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) exposed
via paid APIs, where model internals are unavailable and adversaries rely solely on observable input–output behavior.
3.2. Adversary Goal
The adversary aims to construct natural-language prompts that reliably induce safety violations in the target model
while seeking to remain effective across model updates and exhibiting sufficient structural and semantic diversity. Let
Π ∶→{0, 1} denote an abstract safety indicator, where Π(𝑦) = 1 indicates that 𝑦complies with the safety policy
requirements and Π(𝑦) = 0 indicates 𝑦contains unsafe contents. A prompt 𝑝𝑖∈is regarded as a jailbreak prompt if
Π(𝑀tgt(𝑝𝑖)) = 0, and all such prompts form the jailbreak subset of , defined as jail = { 𝑝𝑖∈| Π(𝑀tgt(𝑝𝑖)) = 0 }.
In this paper, instead of focusing on a single 𝑝𝑖∈jail, the adversary seeks a collection of jailbreak prompts

Although Eq. (2) provides a compact objective, directly searching for the maximizer within the natural-language
prompt space is highly non-trivial. The space is not only combinatorially large, but valid candidates must remain

prompt space in a more directed and linguistically meaningful manner. The next section describes how the initialization,
evaluation, selection, and variation components of EvoJail are designed to operate on the prompt population.
4. Methodology
Given the optimization problem in Eq. (2), EvoJail is designed to navigate the combinatorial and semantically
structured space of natural-language prompts in a directed and linguistically meaningful manner, performing an
iterative evolutionary search that gradually shifts probability mass toward regions of high fitness. As shown in the
Figure 2, the process begins with an initialization phase in which an instruction-fusion mechanism constructs the
initial jailbreak population 𝑃0 by embedding harmful objectives into structurally coherent benign task instructions.
This yields a syntactically valid and semantically grounded set of candidate prompts, mitigating the fragmentation
typically observed under random text initialization.
For each subsequent generation 𝑔≥0, EvoJail updates the population 𝑃𝑔through a sequence of selection,
variation, and evaluation operations designed to jointly optimize attack effectiveness and prompt diversity. An
elite–hybrid evolutionary strategy is designed and implemented in the selection stage, to retain high-fitness individuals
while preserving stochastic exploration across alternative semantic modes. The variation stage introduces controlled
linguistic and structural modifications through LLM-guided crossover and mutation, enabling movement across distant
regions of that are difficult to explore using low-level token perturbations. Finally, newly generated prompts are
evaluated using the safety-risk scorer 𝑆(⋅) and the diversity scorer 𝐷(⋅∣𝑃𝑔) to obtain updated fitness values, forming
the next population 𝑃𝑔+1.
This evolutionary process provides a mechanism for searching the natural-language prompt space under black-box
constraints and for progressively identifying prompts with large values of 𝐹(𝑝), enabling EvoJail to progressively refine
jailbreak prompts even when the underlying model and scoring oracles are non-differentiable. Sections 4.1∼4.4 detail
each component of the EvoJail framework and Algorithm 1 summarizes the overall procedure.

4.3. Elite-Hybrid Evolutionary Strategy
The fitness landscape for jailbreak prompt optimization is highly non-stationary due to stochastic LLM decoding
and evolving safety policies. To ensure stable progress in such environments, EvoJail adopts an elite–hybrid
evolutionary strategy designed to enforce both (i) consistent exploitation of high-performing prompts and (ii) sustained
exploration of semantically diverse regions of the prompt space.
(1) Fitness stabilization via elitism. Given population size 𝑁and elitism rate 𝛼, EvoJail preserves the highest-
scoring 𝑁× 𝛼individuals, bypassing the crossover and mutation operations:

progressively stronger and more diverse attacks.

4.4. LLM-Based Crossover and Mutation Operators
Optimizing natural-language prompts differs fundamentally from classical evolutionary search over binary or
continuous encodings. Token-level perturbations break linguistic coherence, distort adversarial intent, and often push
candidate prompts outside the feasible semantic manifold of . As a result, traditional crossover and mutation operators
are insufficient for navigating the discrete, high-dimensional, and heavily constrained prompt space. To address this
challenge, EvoJail introduces a family of LLM-guided variation operators that leverage the generative and semantic-
editing capabilities of LLMs to produce linguistically coherent yet structurally diverse candidates. Specifically, EvoJail
defines LLM-based variation operators including:
𝑀edit = {𝑀cross, 𝑀mut
},

where 𝑀cross denotes the crossover operator, and the remaining operators represent mutations at different linguistic
granularities. Each operator employs an auxiliary LLM with the prompt template in Appendix A.4, to (i) preserve the
harmful intent embedded in the prompt while (ii) introducing controlled variation at multiple linguistic granularities to
explore both local and global neighborhoods of the prompt space. This design enables EvoJail to perform semantics-
aware exploration, which is essential for discovering high-diversity jailbreak prompts in a black-box setting. This
section will provide a detailed introduction to the crossover and mutation operators used in EvoJail.
4.4.1. Crossover Operator
Given two parent prompts 𝑝𝑖and 𝑝𝑗, EvoJail applies a semantic crossover operator defined as
𝑝new = 𝑀cross(𝑝𝑖, 𝑝𝑗),
(13)
where the editing LLM synthesizes a coherent instruction that integrates structural components of both parents while
maintaining the embedded harmful objective. Unlike token-level recombination, which disrupts linguistic validity,
𝑀cross performs high-level semantic recombination that enables EvoJail to explore novel compositional modes.
4.4.2. Mutation Operator
The purpose of mutation operations is to locally modify a single individual to generate a new individual with a
higher fitness. Unlike crossover operations, mutation focuses more on fine-tuning the details of an individual rather than
performing large-scale combinations. Mutation can be viewed as a refinement process aimed at altering specific parts of
an individual to make it more diverse and increase the chances of bypassing defense mechanisms in the model. EvoJail
instantiates a family of hierarchical mutation operators 𝑀mut ∈{𝑀word, 𝑀sent, 𝑀sem, 𝑀struct}. For an input prompt
𝑝𝑖, a mutated candidate is obtained via 𝑀mut(𝑝𝑖). Each operator is prompted to preserve the core harmful intent of the
original instruction while introducing controlled variation at a specific granularity level (lexical, syntactic, semantic,
or structural).

• Word-level mutation 𝑀word perturbs lexical choices by replacing selected tokens with synonyms or semantically
related expressions.
• Sentence-level mutation 𝑀sent achieves diverse expressions by altering the sentence structure of the prompt. For
example, it may involve converting active voice to passive voice or rephrasing declarative sentences into interrogative
sentences.
• Semantic-level mutation 𝑀sem introduces higher-level paraphrases via metaphors, technical terminology, or
euphemisms, making harmful content more obfuscated yet semantically aligned. For instance, academic terminology,
technical terms, or more ambiguous language can be used to replace sensitive expressions, making the prompt content
harder to identify.
• Structural-level mutation 𝑀struct achieves variation by adjusting the overall structure of the prompt. For
example, it may involve splitting a long sentence into multiple concise short sentences or breaking a request into several
sub-questions. In specific scenarios, symbolic processing can also be introduced, such as using encoding, abbreviations,
or character substitutions to conceal key information.

6. Broader Implications and Responsible AI Considerations
Theoretical implications. From a theoretical perspective, this work offers a systematic view of automated jailbreak
generation by conceptualizing it as a dynamic and multi-objective search process rather than a collection of isolated
prompt-construction heuristics. Beyond the jailbreak setting, this work provides a methodological reference for related
studies by suggesting that a class of prompt-centered research problems under black-box constraints could be viewed

through the lens of iterative search and optimization. This perspective offers a flexible theoretical reference for future
studies seeking to systematically explore and optimize prompts without relying on gradient information or fixed
templates.
Practical implications. From a practical standpoint, EvoJail can serve as an automated tool for continuously probing
LLM safety across model updates and diverse application contexts. Its ability to generate adaptive and field-aware
jailbreak prompts makes it suitable for supporting red-teaming, safety benchmarking, and the construction of more
diverse adversarial datasets, thereby helping practitioners identify latent vulnerabilities and improve the robustness of
real-world LLM deployments.
Ethical considerations. This study investigates automated jailbreak prompt generation with the primary goal of
supporting LLM safety evaluation and robustness improvement. EvoJail is designed and evaluated in controlled
research settings, and all experiments are conducted on publicly accessible models without targeting specific users,
services, or sensitive data. We recognize that vulnerability discovery carries dual-use risks, where malicious actors may
leverage research findings to refine attacks. To address this, we adhere to responsible disclosure principles and have not
published any generated jailbreaks at this time. Where appropriate, relevant vulnerabilities will be reported to affected
model providers prior to the publication of the paper, allowing them sufficient time for remediation. In addition, prior
to conducting experiments, we carefully review and comply with the usage policies of each LLM provider. For tests
that may approach the boundaries of these terms, we always prioritize system security, transparency, and compliance.
7. Conclusion
This paper presents EvoJail, a novel evolutionary framework for generating highly effective jailbreak prompts
against LLMs. By integrating obfuscation-inspired initialization, LLM-guided crossover, and multi-level mutation
strategies within a dual-objective evolutionary process, EvoJail enables the generation of jailbreak prompts with
improved adaptability to model updates and enhanced diversity in attack patterns. Experimental results demonstrate
that EvoJail achieves state-of-the-art jailbreak success rates on multiple safety-finetuned models, while also signif-
icantly increasing prompt diversity, improving by more than 5.6% compared to existing methods. Compared with
heuristic jailbreak methods, our approach requires relatively longer runtime; however, compared with evolutionary
jailbreak methods, it can generate effective jailbreak prompts more efficiently. These improvements translate into
stronger attack generalization and greater red-teaming value. Such research provides a practical and extensible tool for
safety evaluation and model training, laying a foundation for the development of more powerful and safe AI systems.
Future directions include its application in multilingual settings, real-time adversarial adaptation, and integration into
automated LLM auditing pipelines.
